The Fox and the Grapes

A hungry fox wandered into a vineyard late one summer afternoon. High on a trellis hung bunches of dark purple grapes, so ripe they seemed ready to burst. The fox crouched, sprang, and snapped at the nearest bunch, but his jaws closed on empty air. He backed up, took a running start, and leapt again, higher this time, yet still fell short. Again and again he jumped until his legs ached and his tongue hung out.

At last he sat down, looked up at the grapes with his nose in the air, and said to no one in particular, "I don't know why I bothered. Anyone can see those grapes are sour. I wouldn't eat them if they fell into my mouth." And he trotted off, pretending he had never wanted them at all.

Moral: It is easy to scorn what you cannot have.
