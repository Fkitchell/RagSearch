The Oak and the Reed

A tall oak grew beside a river where reeds swayed at the water's edge. "How weak you are," the oak said to a reed. "The smallest breeze bends you over. Look at me. I stand straight and firm against any wind." The reed answered quietly, "I bend, but I do not break."

That night a great storm came roaring down the valley. The reeds bowed low and let the wind pass over them. The oak stood stiff and proud and fought the gale with all its strength, until with a terrible crack it was torn up by the roots and flung into the river. In the morning the reeds stood up again, unharmed.

Moral: It is better to bend than to break.
